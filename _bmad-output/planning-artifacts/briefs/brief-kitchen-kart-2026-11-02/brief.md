# Kitchen Kart — Game Brief

> `[ASSUMPTION]` marks a recommendation not yet explicitly confirmed. Change it in the GDD if wrong.

## Vision
**One sentence:** Study the map during the countdown, then race rival toy karts for the one milkshake on the board and deliver the order before the clock turns red.

**Core fantasy:** being the sharpest, fastest delivery driver in town, outthinking rivals before the race and outdriving (or out-ramming) them during it.

**Emotional arc per order:** tension (countdown, planning) → scramble (contested pickups) → panic (clock white → light orange → light red) → triumph, or "one more."

## Target players
- **Primary:** Gauntlet cohort mates and Game Week judges, together in one room, on laptops. Many games to judge, so Kitchen Kart must be readable and fun within 10 seconds, with no tutorial.
- **Secondary:** anyone with the link, playing solo against bots.
- **Out of scope (v1):** mobile, online remote play.

## Core fundamentals
**Genre:** top-down arcade racing + delivery (Game Week genre: *Multiplayer Racing*).

**Core loop: one shared order at a time**
1. **Countdown:** players spawn at random points; the full map is visible. The top HUD shows the order (e.g. 1 burger + 1 fries + 1 milkshake, as icons) and the color-coded customer to deliver to. The order stays on screen until completed.
2. **Scramble:** on GO, race for items. Spawns are random, but the items' center of mass starts roughly equidistant from every player. Items are scarce: e.g. 2 burgers, 2 fries, **1 milkshake**; the second milkshake spawns after a delay. Some items fade after 5 s and instantly respawn elsewhere.
3. **Deny:** players may hoard extra items to starve opponents. **Carry limit = items in the order + 2.**
4. **Ram:** hitting a rival knocks items off their stack onto the road; anyone can collect them.
5. **Deliver:** first kart to reach the customer with the full order scores. New order → back to 1. Repeat until the round clock ends.

**Order items:** burger, fries, milkshake, ketchup. At most 3 items per order. `[ASSUMPTION]`

**Pillars**
1. **Plan, then panic.** Every order opens with a visible-map countdown.
2. **Scarcity breeds rivalry.** There's never enough of the key item; someone has to lose it.
3. **Pressure you can see.** The clock shifts white → light orange → light red.
4. **Instant "one more."** Rematch in under 2 s; rank and personal best on every end screen.

**Supporting mechanics:** drifty arcade handling (prototype exists); combo multiplier for consecutive deliveries `[ASSUMPTION]`; 60 s rounds `[ASSUMPTION]`.

## Modes
- **Vs bots:** 1 human vs 1-3 bots.
- **Local multiplayer:** up to 4 humans in the same room: 2 on one keyboard (WASD / arrows) + gamepads (browser Gamepad API). Bots fill empty karts.
- **Leaderboards (all-time, online, shared across laptops):** two boards, **Multiplayer** and **Vs Bots**, kept separate so scores stay comparable. Backend `[ASSUMPTION]` Supabase free tier.

**Differentiator:** the **planning countdown + deliberate scarcity + hoarding/ramming** combo. Each order is a small strategy-then-execution duel, not just a race.

## Scope & MVP
- **Team:** 1 developer (first game) + Claude Code, using BMad Game Dev Studio.
- **Timeline:** Mon-Thu, due Thursday midnight; judged Friday morning.
- **Platform:** laptop web browser; one public link; no install.
- **Hard constraint:** all visuals and audio generated by us.
- **Tech:** HTML5 canvas (prototype exists); static hosting (GitHub/Cloudflare Pages); small leaderboard backend.

**MVP, in priority order**
1. Core loop: countdown → scarce/fading spawns → hoard → ram → deliver → next order, with color-shifting clock.
2. Local multiplayer for 2 on keyboard.
3. Bots that compete for and hoard items.
4. Online leaderboards (Multiplayer + Vs Bots).
5. Cohesive chunky-toy art, music, SFX, title screen.

**Stretch:** gamepads for players 3-4, combo multiplier, extra maps.

**Risks**
- Bots feel dumb or unfair → prototype by Tuesday.
- Art not cohesive → lock the style frame Monday.
- Spawn fairness math (equidistant centroid) feels unfair in practice → playtest early, tune.
- Open leaderboard can be spammed → acceptable for a jam.

## Content & direction
- **Art:** **chunky toy.** Bright, rounded, toy cars on a play mat; saturated primaries, soft drop shadows, thick shapes readable at a glance with 4 karts on screen.
- **Setting:** a toy town of fast-food shops; customers color-coded to match HUD.
- **Narrative:** none. The order ticket is the story.
- **Content:** 1 map, 4 item types, 2-4 karts, ~60 s rounds; replayability from random spawns and orders.
- **Audio:** bouncy looping track that speeds up as the clock turns red; punchy pickup, deliver, ram, and steal SFX.
